What the modes show

Process: each ring vs the die average, in ppm. This is manufacturing variation, frozen into this particular chip.

Change: each ring vs the last heater-off frames. Turn the heater on and watch the die slow down (IR drop) and then drift as it heats.

Temperature: per-ring temperature, once each ring has been fitted against the XADC. That happens by itself after the die has cooled through at least 8 °C. It's valid on heater-off frames only.

IR drop: the heater goes off, full on and off again in about 3 s. The extra slowdown under load maps where the core supply sags.

Supply-noise scope

The droop monitor's edge position, captured on chip at up to 25 MS/s (16,384 samples in block RAM), then dumped to the Pi. A lower position means a slower path, so the trace shows core-supply noise as seen from inside the die.

Chip fingerprint

The die map's process variation is unique to this piece of silicon. Each ring gives one bit (faster or slower than the die median): a 64-bit ID. The 32 rings furthest from the median form a steadier key. It's a ring-oscillator PUF, the same idea used to give chips an unclonable identity. Here it's stable for a given bitstream but changes with each rebuild, because routing near each ring shifts its frequency slightly.

Bit flips vs die temperature

Heat the die and some bits near the median flip, because the rings' temperature responses differ slightly. Those flips are why real PUFs pick stable bits and use error correction.

How it's wired

A Raspberry Pi CM4 sits next to the FPGA. It reads the FPGA over two UARTs (the analog lab and the TRNG) and reads the XADC sensor over JTAG. It also runs this page, the safety limits and the self-calibration of the ring oscillators against the XADC.

Safety: the heater cuts out at 72 °C and every session ends after 10 minutes. The FPGA also turns the heater off by itself if the Pi goes quiet, and its own over-temperature alarm is armed. Everything is built with open-source tools (yosys, nextpnr-xilinx, Project X-Ray).

Updates every second. History is sampled every 10 s and kept for 30 days.
